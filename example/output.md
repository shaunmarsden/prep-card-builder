Source: [inputs.md](inputs.md).

## Source Ledger

- Recruiter screen notes (direct statement from Jamie): mentions the same ticketing tool the team uses
- CV: four years at Northgate Systems in a support role, dates confirmed
- LinkedIn (public background): two posts about "AI in customer support" as a general topic

## Confirmed Context

Four years in a support role at Northgate Systems. Direct familiarity with the same ticketing tool the team already uses, confirmed in the recruiter screen.

## Public Background

Jamie has posted about AI in customer support twice on LinkedIn. This shows an interest in the topic; it is not evidence of hands-on incident response experience, which is what this interview specifically needs to assess.

## Assumptions to Test

Four years in "a support role" does not by itself confirm live production incident experience; the CV's job title does not specify this, and it needs to be asked directly rather than assumed from tenure alone.

## Unknowns

Whether Jamie has actually handled a live production incident, as opposed to ticket triage, is not established by anything gathered so far. This is the single most important gap given the role's core requirement.

## Working Hypothesis

Jamie's confirmed ticketing tool familiarity is a genuine plus; whether the underlying four years included real incident response, the role's actual critical requirement, is untested and should be the focus of this interview.

## The Conversation

**Opening:** "Thanks for making time again. Today I want to dig into the hands-on side of incident response specifically, since that's the core of this role."

**Questions:**
1. "Walk me through the last live production incident you were directly involved in resolving."
2. "What was your specific role versus the rest of the team's during that incident?"
3. "How did you handle a case where the initial diagnosis turned out to be wrong?"
4. "What's changed about how you approach an incident since you started in support?"

**If the hypothesis holds** (genuine incident experience confirmed): move to discussing on-call expectations for this specific team.

**If it does not** (experience turns out to be ticket triage only, not live incidents): be honest that this is the role's core requirement, and explore whether there is a genuine path to closing that gap quickly, or whether this is a mismatch worth naming directly.

**If the interview gets cut short:** a short follow-up message confirming interest and asking Jamie to describe one specific incident in writing, rather than leaving the core question unanswered.
